# Watch-list: Unix processes and signals

Attach when the diff spawns or supervises child processes, handles signals, process groups, or PIDs, writes to pipes or sockets from long-lived processes, exposes a control socket, or parses subprocess output.

Apply after coverage enumeration, using the common worker brief's reporting format and the supplied evidence rules.

- **UP1 SIGPIPE and blocking writes.** Can a write to a closed pipe or socket kill or freeze the process? **Verify:** close the reader mid-write and stall the reader. Leads: `MSG_NOSIGNAL` works only on `send()` to sockets (macOS uses `SO_NOSIGPIPE`), so pipe writers need SIGPIPE ignored or blocked. Handle `EPIPE`, and bound or make non-blocking any write to a peer that can stall.
- **UP2 Inherited signal state.** Are the signal mask and ignored dispositions reset between fork and exec? **Verify:** read `SigBlk` and `SigIgn` from `/proc/<child>/status`.
- **UP3 Reaping.** Is `waitpid` returning 0 under `WNOHANG` treated as "still running" rather than as drained? Are `ECHILD` and adopted descendants (subreaper) handled, with exits reaped even when no drain was requested? **Verify:** spawn a grandchild that outlives its parent, and an early exit before the client subscribes.
- **UP4 Process-group drain.** Does drain wait until the whole group is gone, not just until the leader is reaped, and escalate TERM to KILL for members that ignore TERM? Does the signal still reach the real child after a wrapper change? **Verify:** run a grandchild that ignores TERM and keeps writing.
- **UP5 PID reuse.** Are processes identified by pidfd, start time, or a nonce rather than a bare PID? **Verify:** find every signal-by-PID path and every PID parser, and check each one for reuse between observation and signal.
- **UP6 Control-socket authentication.** Can only the intended client authenticate, so that other processes with the same credentials fail, including its descendants whether they fork only or fork and exec? **Verify:** name the evidence that distinguishes the client, then connect from a forked child that does not exec and from one that does, expecting rejection. Leads: socket permissions and UID-only `SO_PEERCRED` checks admit same-UID processes. A secret on a close-on-exec fd is protected only across exec, because forked children inherit it until they exec, so close it before forking untrusted children. Other options are a socketpair handed only to the client, or a peer-PID match guarded against reuse (pidfd, `SO_PEERPIDFD`, start time).
- **UP7 Framing.** Are frames length-prefixed or escaped, so that child output containing a delimiter cannot forge records? Are partial, overlapping, and legacy frames rejected? Does one error frame fail only its own waiter? **Verify:** have the child print a fake exit record, then send a partial frame and an error frame while several waiters are pending.
- **UP8 Buffer arithmetic.** Are offsets and lengths recomputed after a reset, with no unsigned underflow in `memmove` or copy sizes? **Verify:** fuzz the consume loop under ASan or UBSan.
- **UP9 Every spawn is supervised.** Do all mutating subprocesses go through the supervisor and its drain, with no direct spawn side doors? **Verify:** search for `spawn`, `exec`, `Command::new`, and `fork` and trace each one.
- **UP10 Flag injection in argv.** Can a value in argv be read as an option? **Verify:** apply input watch-list IT1 to every spawn site.
- **UP11 Stale ownership metadata.** After the owner is SIGKILLed or OOM-killed, or dies between discovery and capture, does recovery handle its leftover pid file, socket, or receipt instead of failing closed forever? Is the identity witness strong (for example, `/proc/<pid>/exe` reads `(deleted)` after an in-place upgrade)? **Verify:** kill the owner at each step, then start a new instance, including after replacing the binary.
- **UP12 One-shot events are latched.** When a one-shot kernel observation (kqueue `EVFILT_PROC`, eventfd, `waitpid` status) is combined with another check (`has_exited() && other()`), is the observation latched so that a failed second check doesn't lose it? **Verify:** find each read of a one-shot source and make the follow-up check fail once.
- **UP13 Opening instead of stat.** Does replacing a stat or type check with `open` change the semantics for execute-only (`0111`), write-only, FIFO, or device files (failing, or blocking on a FIFO swapped in)? **Verify:** test each file type and mode. Prefer `fstatat` or `O_PATH` for existence and type checks, and `O_NONBLOCK` where a FIFO is possible; check every other open, verify, and rollback path for the same pattern.
- **UP14 Path confinement and TOCTOU.** Does every path resolved under a trusted root end at a file of the expected type beneath that root, under the symlink policy the contract sets, even while components are swapped for symlinks or FIFOs? **Verify:** name the confinement mechanism and the symlink policy separately, and state whether concurrent directory renames are in the threat model. Leads: `openat2` with `RESOLVE_BENEATH` or `RESOLVE_IN_ROOT` confines (Linux). `RESOLVE_NO_SYMLINKS` and `O_NOFOLLOW` set symlink policy only, and `O_NOFOLLOW` covers only the final component. A walk one component at a time must reject `..`, empty, and absolute components. Use `O_NONBLOCK` where a FIFO is possible and check the type with `fstat` on the opened fd. Probe with an intermediate symlink, a final symlink, `..`, and a FIFO, and with a directory-rename race if renames are in scope.
- **UP15 Signal waiting works on each supported OS.** Does a blocked signal with an ignored or default-ignored disposition become pending for `sigwait` on the target platform, or can it be discarded before the waiter sees it? **Verify:** use a bounded native probe on each supported OS, including macOS when targeted: set the exact disposition and mask, send before and during the wait, and assert delivery or the contract's fallback. Check pinned platform documentation; do not extrapolate Linux results to other kernels.
