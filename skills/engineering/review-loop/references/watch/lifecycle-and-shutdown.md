# Watch-list: Lifecycle, cancellation, and shutdown

Attach when the diff touches servers, workers, dispatchers, supervisors, long-lived connections, timers, heartbeats, deadlines, cancellation, drain, or shutdown.

Apply after coverage enumeration, using the common worker brief's reporting format and the supplied evidence rules.

- **LS1 Shutdown drains first.** Do `stop()` and shutdown await in-flight work (dispatch cycles, callbacks, reservations, finalizations) before disconnecting the database, queues, or clients, and without detaching callbacks whose finalization has not settled? **Verify:** start a slow operation, call shutdown, and assert that the operation completes or is durably recorded, with no pool-closed errors.
- **LS2 Drain sees late arrivals.** After a drain starts, or after its promise resolves, are late spawns, acks, and capability or lease requests either rejected or awaited? **Verify:** look for memoized drain promises and "closing" flags. Start new work after the first drain resolves, and during teardown after a successful callback.
- **LS3 Deadlines match reality.** When a deadline fires after work was admitted, does the reported outcome match what actually happened? Does the deadline also bound every stage (follow-up queries, subprocesses, lock waits), rather than only the first? **Verify:** make the callback slower than the deadline and compare the reported outcome with the durable effects. Check each awaited stage for a timeout.
- **LS4 Omitted timeouts are still bounded.** When an optional timeout is omitted, is the wait still bounded, and do in-process queues ahead of a bounded wait also have a bound? **Verify:** find call sites that pass no timeout and trace the default. Look for queues or gates that sit in front of the bounded operation.
- **LS5 Background errors are routed.** Is a rejection or throw in a heartbeat, timer, interval, or consumer caught and routed into the retry or state machine, without crashing the runtime and without bypassing durable retry? **Verify:** make each background task throw and observe the process and the persisted state.
- **LS6 Cancellation is handled at every stage.** Is an already-cancelled signal honored before any dispatch? Does cancellation mid-upload or mid-spawn release references and leave a settled state, and is a late ack after cancel harmless? **Verify:** pass a pre-aborted signal, cancel at each await point, and inspect leftovers.
- **LS7 Revocation reaches live sessions.** Do all supported long-lived connection types and handshakes awaiting I/O honor revocation, rather than relying only on blocking new credentials? **Verify:** enumerate connection type × revocation event; revoke during the handshake's database write and during a live session, then check that protected activity stops according to the contract and that the old session cannot evict its successor.
- **LS8 Waits re-probe the real condition.** Does each wait loop re-probe the condition it needs instead of assuming one outcome (waiting for exit when the peer may be mid-transition and never exit)? When the situation changes during the wait (a successor appears, becomes incompatible, or becomes unreachable), does the code report the current outcome at once instead of spinning to the deadline and returning a stale error? **Verify:** for each wait, list the peer's possible transitions and drive each one during the wait, checking both the exit condition and the error returned.
- **LS9 End markers on every exit.** Are end markers, releases, and state resets (completion record, lock release, in-progress flag, span close) written on every exit path: panic or exception, early return, `?`, and cancellation (a future dropped at an await)? **Verify:** list the exits between start and end markers and force each one. Prefer scope guards (RAII, `finally`, `defer`, `using`) over explicit calls at each exit.
- **LS10 Cancellation settles real pending work.** Does a deadline or cancel settle the caller and release ownership safely while a real read, child process, or connection is still pending? **Verify:** stall real I/O or use a child that delays exit, cancel it, measure settlement against the deadline, and inspect ownership until the underlying work actually ends. Deliver a late result after a successor starts. A mock whose `abort()` or `kill()` settles synchronously cannot verify this boundary; mark it unverified if a real probe is unavailable.
